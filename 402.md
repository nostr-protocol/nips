NIP-402
=======

L402 Authentication for Paid Relays
-----------------------------------

`draft` `optional` `relay`

This NIP defines a binding of the [L402 protocol](https://github.com/lightninglabs/L402)
(Lightning HTTP 402) to the nostr relay protocol, letting a relay charge for
access with a self-verifying, payment-bound bearer credential. The payment itself
remains an out-of-band Lightning payment; what moves in-band over the WebSocket
is the invoice and the proof of payment, so the relay verifies payment from the
credential rather than from a separate payment page and a pubkey-whitelist
database.

## Motivation

Paid relays today assemble three separate pieces: fee advertisement in
[NIP-11](11.md), an out-of-band payment on a web or LNURL page, and
[NIP-42](42.md) authentication so the relay can recognize the key it has
whitelisted. Nothing in the protocol carries a proof of payment; the relay keeps
a private `pubkey -> paid` database that an operator must populate out of band.

This arrangement has several weaknesses:

  - **No payment proof in the protocol.** Authentication and payment are separate
    gates: NIP-42 only proves which pubkey is connected, not that it has paid. So
    a client can pass authentication and still have its request refused because
    that pubkey has not paid — and nothing in the protocol lets it prove payment
    to clear that second gate; the relay learns who has paid only from its own
    out-of-band database.
  - **Coarse, all-or-nothing access.** A whitelisted key typically unlocks every
    restricted resource at once; there is no standard way to scope access to
    particular kinds, a validity window, or a usage budget.
  - **Hostile to ephemeral keys.** Per-pubkey subscriptions do not fit flows like
    [NIP-17](17.md) private messages or
    [Marmot](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot) group messaging, which
    publish each event under a fresh ephemeral key, leaving nothing to subscribe.
  - **Not portable.** The paid status lives in one relay's database and cannot be
    presented elsewhere.

L402 addresses these directly. A relay issues a Lightning invoice together with a
macaroon that commits to the invoice's payment hash. The client pays, obtains the
preimage, and presents the macaroon plus the preimage as a bearer credential. The
relay verifies the credential statelessly — the preimage proves the invoice was
paid, and the macaroon's caveats scope what the credential grants — without
consulting any per-pubkey database. By default the credential is not tied to a
nostr identity, so it works with ephemeral keys and can be reused across
connections until it expires or is revoked
(a relay can optionally bind it to a pubkey; see [Binding to a single
party](#binding-to-a-single-party)).

## Background: L402

L402 (formerly LSAT) is an open protocol from Lightning Labs for paying for and
authenticating access to services using the Lightning Network. A full description
lives in the canonical specification; the parts relevant here are:

  - A **macaroon** is a bearer credential whose authority is restricted by a chain
    of **caveats** and protected by an HMAC chain keyed on a root key the relay
    controls.
    Any holder may add further restricting caveats without the root key; no holder
    can remove a caveat.
  - The macaroon's **identifier** is `version || payment_hash || token_id` — a
    format version, the Lightning invoice's **payment hash**, and a `token_id` (in
    canonical L402 a stable per-user id; inert here, since the per-credential
    payment hash already makes each identifier unique — see
    [Payment proof and delegation](#payment-proof-and-delegation)) — so it commits
    the macaroon to that invoice. The
    32-byte **preimage** revealed by paying the invoice is the proof of payment:
    `SHA-256(preimage)` equals the committed payment hash.
  - Verification needs no payment-settlement state: each macaroon's root key is
    derived from a relay master secret and the macaroon identifier, so the relay
    re-derives it at verification time rather than storing one per credential. It
    recomputes the macaroon's HMAC chain, checks the preimage against the committed
    payment hash, and evaluates the caveats against the request — with no
    settled-invoice or paid-pubkey lookup.

L402 is specified for HTTP (using the `402 Payment Required` status and the
`WWW-Authenticate` / `Authorization` headers) and for gRPC (which, lacking HTTP
status codes, carries the same challenge and credential in gRPC metadata and
trailers). The nostr relay protocol is a third transport with the same property
as gRPC — there is no per-message HTTP exchange once the WebSocket is open — so
this NIP carries the L402 challenge and credential in nostr protocol messages.
The macaroon format, caveats, and preimage proof are reused from L402 unchanged;
this NIP defines the nostr transport binding and a few deliberate deviations (see
[Differences from canonical L402](#differences-from-canonical-l402)). The Lightning payment
itself still settles over the Lightning Network; what this binding carries
in-band is the challenge (the invoice and macaroon) and the proof of payment
(the preimage), so the relay verifies payment from the message instead of from a
side database.

## Differences from canonical L402

The credential is standard L402: the macaroon format, the caveat mechanism, the
preimage proof, and the `<base64(macaroon)>:<hex(preimage)>` token are unchanged, so
any L402 library can parse, attenuate, and re-serialize a macaroon issued here.
Verifying its signature, though, requires this relay's derived root key, so a
credential is verified by the issuing relay (or a sibling that shares its master
secret), not by an unrelated L402 verifier. This NIP differs from the canonical
specification in three places:

  - **Transport.** L402 is specified over HTTP and gRPC; this NIP adds a binding
    to the nostr relay WebSocket protocol, carrying the challenge and credential in
    `L402` messages and the `payment-required:` prefix rather than in HTTP or gRPC
    headers.
  - **Fail-closed caveats.** The L402 macaroon specification says an unrecognized
    caveat condition MUST be skipped, not rejected. This NIP reverses that: a relay
    MUST reject a credential carrying a condition it does not recognize (see
    [Caveats](#caveats)). Skipping is safe when caveats are optional forward-compatibility
    hints, but here the caveats are the access control, so a skipped caveat would
    silently widen access.
  - **Invoice format.** The invoice MAY be BOLT 11 or BOLT 12; the macaroon commits
    to each invoice's payment hash either way. BOLT 12 additionally enables an
    optional payment receipt that binds the payment to the payer's key (see
    [Binding to a single party](#binding-to-a-single-party)).

## Advertising support

A relay that implements this NIP SHOULD advertise it in its [NIP-11](11.md)
document:

  - list this NIP in `supported_nips` as the integer `402`;
  - set `limitation.payment_required` to `true` only if the relay gates the whole
    connection; a relay that gates only some actions SHOULD leave it unset and rely
    on the in-band `payment-required:` challenge, since NIP-11 defines the flag as a
    connection-level gate;
  - describe its pricing in the `fees` object as already defined by NIP-11.

Clients can therefore learn that a relay is paid, and roughly what it costs,
before connecting. The authoritative invoice and macaroon are always delivered
in-band (see below), so the NIP-11 document is advisory for pricing only.

This NIP uses the decimal identifier `402` — the HTTP "Payment Required" status that
L402 is named for — which also lets it appear in `supported_nips`: NIP-11 specifies
that field as a list of integer NIP numbers and cannot represent the repository's
newer two-character hexadecimal identifiers (`5A`, `7D`, `A0`, …). The number is a
deliberate departure from the two-character convention, chosen both for its meaning
and to work around the `supported_nips` limitation. Generalizing `supported_nips` to
carry hex identifiers would remove the constraint and is a worthwhile NIP-11 fix.

## The `L402` message

This NIP defines a new client-relay protocol message, `L402`, used in both
directions, mirroring the shape of `AUTH` in [NIP-42](42.md).

When sent by a relay, it is a **challenge** carrying a macaroon and the invoice
that must be paid to use it:

```
["L402", <macaroon>, <invoice>]
```

  - `<macaroon>` is the macaroon, base64-encoded (RFC 4648).
  - `<invoice>` is a BOLT 11 or BOLT 12 payment request issued by the relay. The
    relay MUST be the issuer, so that it knows the payment hash committed in the
    macaroon and can verify the preimage statelessly.

When sent by a client, it is a **credential** presenting the paid macaroon and the
preimage obtained by paying the invoice:

```
["L402", <token>]
```

where `<token>` is the L402 credential string

```
<base64(macaroon)>:<hex(preimage)>
```

exactly as in L402 over HTTP: the macaroon is base64-encoded (RFC 4648), the
preimage is hex-encoded, and the two are joined by a single `:`.

`L402` messages sent by clients MUST be answered with a dedicated `L402-OK`
message — a response verb distinct from the `OK` that acks an `EVENT`, so the two
never share a correlation namespace. It mirrors the shape of `OK`: a handle, a
boolean status, a human-readable message, and an optional trailing parameter. The
second element is the **credential handle**: the lowercase hex-encoded `SHA-256` of
the macaroon identifier (not of the whole macaroon). Both sides can compute it; it
is stable across attenuation (adding caveats changes the macaroon bytes but not its
identifier), and a relay keys any per-credential metered counter on this same
value, so a client cannot reset a budget by attenuating:

```
["L402-OK", <sha256(identifier)>, true, ""]
```

A client correlates the response to the credential it sent by matching this handle
against the one it computed. Because `L402-OK` is its own message type, a client
never has to disambiguate it from an `EVENT` ack, and a generic `OK`-by-event-id
router is unaffected.

A rejection carries `false` and a machine-readable prefix, whether it rejects a
credential presented with `L402` (answered by `L402-OK`) or a later gated operation
(answered by that operation's own `OK`, `CLOSED`, or `NEG-ERR`). The prefix says
why. `payment-required:`, with a fresh challenge in the optional trailing parameter,
is used whenever a new payment would grant access: the client has no valid
credential (a macaroon that does not verify, is unknown, or has been revoked), an
unpaid invoice, an expired `valid_until`, or an exhausted consumable budget.
`restricted:` is used when the credential verifies and is paid but is unusable as
presented — the request falls outside its caveats (paying the same credential would
not help), it is presented before its `valid_after`, or it carries a caveat
condition the relay does not recognize (rejected fail-closed at verification). Other
conditions use their own prefix: `auth-required:` for an `authors=self` credential
before NIP-42 AUTH, and `rate-limited:` / `subscription-limit-reached:` for an
exceeded `rate` / `subscriptions` ceiling (see [Caveats](#caveats)).

The `L402-OK` that rejects a presented credential reports only the
request-independent outcomes — `payment-required:` (with an optional fresh challenge
in its fifth element) or `restricted:`; the request-dependent prefixes (exhausted
budget, `rate`, `subscriptions`, out-of-caveat requests, and `auth-required:` for an
`authors=self` credential before NIP-42 AUTH) appear later on the rejected
operation's own message. A failed `L402-OK` carries the credential handle in its
second element when the relay can compute it, so a client correlates the failure by
handle as it would a success:

```
["L402-OK", <sha256(identifier)>, false, "payment-required: pay to publish here", "{\"macaroon\":\"<base64>\",\"invoice\":\"<invoice>\"}"]
```

When the relay cannot compute the handle (a malformed credential it cannot parse) it
sends an empty string in that position, which is not individually correlatable; a
client awaiting such a failure should submit one `L402` at a time.

## New machine-readable prefixes

This NIP defines two new machine-readable prefixes. `payment-required:` is carried on
the rejection message of any gated operation — `OK` (a rejected `EVENT` write),
`CLOSED` (a rejected `REQ`, `COUNT` per [NIP-45](45.md), or `search` REQ), and
`NEG-ERR` (a rejected `sync`, [NIP-77](77.md), whose error reasons use this same prefix
format) — and also on the `L402-OK` that rejects a presented credential (as above).
`subscription-limit-reached:` is carried only on `CLOSED`, for a `REQ` that would
exceed the `subscriptions` ceiling. They extend the prefixes
defined elsewhere: `auth-required:` (from [NIP-42](42.md)), `restricted:` (standardized
in [NIP-01](01.md) with the already-authenticated-but-not-allowed meaning from
[NIP-42](42.md), which this NIP reuses for a paid credential used outside its
caveats), and `rate-limited:` (from [NIP-01](01.md)):

  - `"payment-required: "` — the client must present a valid `L402` credential to
    fulfill the write or query, and does not yet have one that applies.
  - `"subscription-limit-reached: "` — the credential's `subscriptions` ceiling is
    reached; the client frees a slot by closing a subscription (transient, not a
    payment problem).

A relay MAY deliver the challenge reactively, as the trailing parameter of the
rejecting message, so a client that connects without a stored credential learns
what to pay. The trailing parameter is a JSON-stringified object with `macaroon`
and `invoice` keys — the same two values as a relay-sent `L402` message — placed
where [NIP-01](01.md) puts the `rate-limited` backoff value (the fifth element of
`OK`, the fourth of `CLOSED`):

```
["OK", <event-id>, false, "payment-required: pay to publish here", "{\"macaroon\":\"<base64>\",\"invoice\":\"<invoice>\"}"]
["CLOSED", "sub1", "payment-required: pay to read here", "{\"macaroon\":\"<base64>\",\"invoice\":\"<invoice>\"}"]
```

A relay MAY instead (or additionally) send a proactive `["L402", <macaroon>,
<invoice>]` challenge at any time, exactly as NIP-42 relays may send an `AUTH`
challenge proactively. A rejected `sync` uses this proactive form, since its
`NEG-ERR` rejection has no trailing parameter to carry the challenge.

## Caveats

Access is scoped by the macaroon's caveats, which are UTF-8 `condition=value`
pairs as defined by the L402 macaroon specification. Caveats only ever restrict
authority: repeated conditions MUST be increasingly restrictive, and a relay MUST
reject a credential whose request falls outside any caveat it recognizes.

This NIP defines the caveat conditions below. Because these caveats *are* the
access control, a relay verifying a credential MUST reject any credential that
carries a caveat condition it does not recognize and enforce. This overrides
L402's default, under which an unrecognized caveat is skipped rather than
rejected: skipping is safe when caveats are optional forward-compatibility hints,
but here a skipped caveat would silently widen access. A relay MAY define
conditions beyond those below, provided every relay that accepts the credential
enforces the same set.

A read-scope condition that constrains a `REQ` (`kinds`, `authors`,
`tag_<letter>`, `tag_keys`) applies to **every** filter object in the `REQ`: each
filter MUST independently satisfy it, so a client cannot widen its scope by adding
an unconstrained filter alongside a conforming one. (An empty-value `tag_<letter>` is
the one exception — it is write-only and imposes no read-side per-filter requirement;
see the `tag_<letter>` entry.) These read-scope conditions
apply to the filters of every filter-carrying request: `REQ`, `COUNT`
([NIP-45](45.md)), `search` ([NIP-50](50.md), a `search` field on a `REQ`), and
`sync` ([NIP-77](77.md), whose `NEG-OPEN` carries a NIP-01 filter). `operations`
gates which operation type is allowed; the read-scope conditions gate what its
filters may match.

The conditions fall into two classes. **Scope** conditions need no per-credential
state to enforce — the relay checks them from the request plus its own clock — and
preserve L402's property that a credential is verifiable without any per-credential
database. **Metered** conditions require the relay to keep per-credential state,
trading some of that statelessness for usage-based pricing, and come in three
kinds. The consumable budgets and ceilings keep a per-credential counter, keyed on
the credential's handle (the `SHA-256` of the macaroon identifier) and **aggregated
across every connection that presents it**: a relay MUST maintain one counter per
credential, not one per connection, so a
client cannot multiply a budget, `rate`, or `subscriptions` ceiling by opening
parallel connections. Across a shared-secret cluster this same counter must be
shared between nodes (see [Security considerations](#security-considerations)).
Because a credential is presented concurrently across connections, a relay MUST
check and update each metered counter as a single atomic step per credential handle,
so two simultaneous gated actions cannot both consume the last unit of a budget or
both take the last `subscriptions` slot (for example reserve-then-commit, or a
compare-and-swap on the counter). A gated action that cannot atomically reserve what
it needs is rejected — `payment-required:` for an exhausted budget,
`subscription-limit-reached:` for the `subscriptions` ceiling.
**Consumable budgets** (the `events` and `bytes` families below) are spent over the life of the
credential; when one is exhausted the relay issues a fresh `payment-required:`
challenge, the natural point for the client to top up. **Ceilings**
(`subscriptions`, `rate`) are instantaneous limits that free up again and do not
exhaust the credential: exceeding `rate` is signaled with `rate-limited:` and the
NIP-01 millisecond backoff (in the trailing parameter where the message has one), and exceeding `subscriptions`
with `subscription-limit-reached:` (no backoff — the client frees a slot by closing
a subscription). A **retention
attribute** (`retention`) sets how long events written under the credential are
kept; it is applied at write time rather than spent or capped.

### Scope conditions (stateless)

  - `relay=<url>` — the credential is valid only at this relay URL. Clients SHOULD
    check it, and relays MUST, using the URL normalization of [NIP-42](42.md).
  - `valid_until=<unix-seconds>` — the credential expires at this time; validity is
    half-open, `valid_after <= now < valid_until`, in unix seconds.
  - `valid_after=<unix-seconds>` — the credential is not valid before this time,
    letting a client pre-purchase a future access window. A credential presented
    before `valid_after` is rejected at acceptance with `restricted:` and a message
    naming the time (e.g. `restricted: not valid until <unix-time>`); the client
    waits rather than paying again. This is an acceptance-time check only.
  - `kinds=<comma-separated kind numbers>` — restricts the event kinds the
    credential authorizes: for writes, the `kind` of the `EVENT`; for reads, every
    filter MUST carry a `kinds` field that is a non-empty subset of the allowed set
    (a filter that omits `kinds` matches all kinds and is rejected with
    `restricted:`, so a client cannot omit it to read outside its scope).
  - `operations=<comma-separated>` — restricts which operations the credential
    authorizes. Each incoming message maps to exactly one operation token:
    `publish` (an `EVENT`), `search` (a `REQ` with a `search` field in any filter,
    [NIP-50](50.md)), `read` (any other `REQ`), `count` (a `COUNT`,
    [NIP-45](45.md)), and `sync` (a `NEG-OPEN`, [NIP-77](77.md)); the credential
    MUST list that token. A relay MAY price the more expensive operations, such as
    `search`, `count`, and `sync`, on their own.
  - `authors=<comma-separated pubkeys, or self>` — restricts which author pubkeys
    the credential covers. For writes, the `EVENT`'s `pubkey` MUST be in the set.
    For reads, every filter MUST carry an `authors` field that is a non-empty subset
    of the set (a filter omitting `authors` matches all authors and is rejected with
    `restricted:`). The value `self` resolves to the pubkey(s) authenticated over
    [NIP-42](42.md) on the same connection — for "act only as yourself" (for
    example, read only your own direct messages) — while keeping payment separate
    from authentication. Enforcing `self` requires a completed NIP-42 AUTH: a gated
    action under an `authors=self` credential with no authenticated pubkey is
    answered with `auth-required:` (prompting AUTH); once authenticated, a write
    whose `pubkey` — or a read filter whose `authors` — is not within the
    connection's authenticated pubkeys is `restricted:`.
  - `tag_<letter>=<comma-separated values>` — restricts the credential to events
    involving a given single-letter tag, where `<letter>` is the tag name (for
    example `tag_t=bitcoin,nostr` scopes to the `t` topic tag). For writes, every
    `<letter>` tag the `EVENT` carries MUST have a value in the allowed set, and the
    `EVENT` MUST carry at least one such tag; for
    reads, every filter MUST carry a `#<letter>` tag filter whose values are a
    non-empty subset of the allowed set (a filter omitting it matches regardless of
    that tag and is rejected with `restricted:`), so a client cannot omit the filter
    and read outside its paid scope. An empty value set (`tag_t=`) overrides both rules
    above: the `EVENT` MUST NOT carry a `<letter>` tag at all, so neither the general
    write rule nor the per-filter read rule applies (there is no permitted value to
    require); to stop a credential from filtering reads on a tag letter, omit that
    letter from `tag_keys`. Repeated `tag_<letter>` caveats for the same letter intersect.
  - `tag_keys=<comma-separated letters>` — an allowlist of the tag letters a `REQ`
    may filter on. A `#<letter>` filter for a letter not in the list is rejected.
    Omitting this caveat permits filtering on any tag (the default); `tag_keys=t,p`
    permits only `#t` and `#p`; an empty list (`tag_keys=`) disallows tag filtering
    entirely. It composes with `tag_<letter>`, which further restricts the values
    allowed for a listed letter.
  - `max_event_size=<bytes>` — caps the size of a writable event, measured as the
    UTF-8 byte length of the event's JSON object (the `{...}` event, not the
    enclosing message frame). The `bytes` budgets use this same measure. It bounds
    the storage a single write can consume.

### Metered conditions (stateful)

  - `write_events=<integer>` / `read_events=<integer>` / `events=<integer>` — event
    budgets: `write_events` counts stored writes, `read_events` counts events
    served, and `events` is a combined budget spent by both. A relay may set any
    combination; each present budget is enforced, so a stored write decrements
    `write_events` and `events`, and a served event decrements `read_events` and
    `events`. A write is charged only when the relay newly stores the event; an event
    the relay does not persist — a duplicate (acknowledged with `["OK", <id>, true,
    "duplicate: …"]`) or an ephemeral event (NIP-01 kinds 20000-29999) — is
    acknowledged but not charged, as is a rejected write (`["OK", <id>, false, …]`). A
    unit already spent is not refunded if the event is later superseded or discarded
    (for example by a replaceable or addressable event). An event is
    "served" — and charged — when the relay commits it to a
    subscription's outbound frame, whether it is a stored or a live event, and it is
    not refunded if the client disconnects before reading it. When a read budget is
    exhausted while a subscription is still streaming, the relay stops serving that
    subscription and closes it with
    `["CLOSED", <sub>, "payment-required: ...", <challenge>]` rather than silently
    stopping or serving further events for free. Because only served events are
    charged, a `COUNT` (which returns only a count) and a `sync` (which exchanges
    range fingerprints, not events) spend nothing against the event or byte budgets;
    a relay instead prices each as its own operation — a separate credential scoped
    with `operations` to `count` or `sync`, limited by its own `rate` and priced by
    its own invoice, since no event or byte budget applies to an operation that
    serves no events (see `operations` and `rate`). A `search` is a `REQ` that serves
    events, so it charges per served event like any other read.
  - `write_bytes=<integer>` / `read_bytes=<integer>` / `bytes=<integer>` — the same
    three budgets in bytes, each charged the per-event UTF-8 size defined for
    `max_event_size`, at the same charge points as the event budgets (a write only
    when the event is newly stored, a read when the event is committed to the
    outbound frame).
  - `subscriptions=<integer>` — the maximum number of concurrent subscriptions the
    credential may hold, as in [NIP-11](11.md)'s `max_subscriptions`. The aggregated
    count is reconciled whenever a subscription ends — a client `CLOSE`, a `REQ` that
    reuses an open subscription's id (which replaces it, per [NIP-01](01.md)), a
    relay-sent `CLOSED`, or the connection dropping — so a replaced or ended
    subscription does not keep holding a slot (unlike the consumable budgets, which
    are not refunded).
  - `rate=<count>/<seconds>` — a rate limit bound to the credential: at most `count`
    gated actions per window of `seconds`, where each gated action the credential
    authorizes — one `EVENT`, one `REQ` (plain read or `search`), one `COUNT`, or one
    `sync` — counts as one, aggregated across
    every connection presenting the credential like the other metered counters. The
    relay MAY use any window algorithm (sliding, token-bucket, or fixed) but MUST
    signal an exceeded limit with `rate-limited:`, carrying a millisecond backoff in
    the trailing parameter where the rejecting message has one (`OK`/`CLOSED`); a
    rate-limited `sync` is refused with a `NEG-ERR` `rate-limited:` reason, which has
    no trailing parameter for a backoff, so the client backs off on its own before
    retrying (an absent backoff value is not an instruction to retry immediately). To price
    distinct operations separately, a relay issues separate credentials scoped with
    `operations` — for example a search-only credential — each with its own `rate`
    and invoice (and its own event/byte budgets where the operation serves or stores
    events), rather than metering unlike operations in one shared counter.
  - `retention=<seconds>` — the minimum time the relay commits to retain events
    written under the credential, independent of the credential's own expiry or
    revocation. It does not override NIP-01 replaceable/addressable discard rules.

## Protocol flow

Writing an event to a relay that requires payment:

```
client: ["EVENT", {"id": "012345...", ...}]
relay:  ["OK", "012345...", false, "payment-required: pay to publish here", "{\"macaroon\":\"AGIAJEem...==\",\"invoice\":\"<invoice>\"}"]
        (client pays the invoice and obtains the preimage, e.g. via NIP-47)
client: ["L402", "AGIAJEem...==:38ec9f7c..."]
relay:  ["L402-OK", "9b2c...e1", true, ""]
client: ["EVENT", {"id": "012345...", ...}]
relay:  ["OK", "012345...", true, ""]
```

Reading from a relay that requires payment:

```
client: ["REQ", "sub1", {"kinds": [1]}]
relay:  ["CLOSED", "sub1", "payment-required: pay to read here", "{\"macaroon\":\"AGIAJEem...==\",\"invoice\":\"<invoice>\"}"]
        (client pays the invoice and obtains the preimage)
client: ["L402", "AGIAJEem...==:38ec9f7c..."]
relay:  ["L402-OK", "9b2c...e1", true, ""]
client: ["REQ", "sub1", {"kinds": [1]}]
relay:  ["EVENT", "sub1", {...}]
        ...
```

On a later connection the client re-presents the stored credential without paying
again, as long as it has not expired or been revoked:

```
client: ["L402", "AGIAJEem...==:38ec9f7c..."]
relay:  ["L402-OK", "9b2c...e1", true, ""]
```

## Verification

To accept a client `L402` credential, a relay MUST, in order:

  1. parse the credential into its macaroon and preimage;
  2. derive the macaroon's root key from a relay master secret and the macaroon
     identifier, recompute the HMAC chain, and compare it to the macaroon's
     signature in constant time; a relay that has rotated its secret tries each
     retained secret (current first, then retired — see the rotation note below),
     and a signature that verifies under none was not minted by this relay. Also
     reject if the credential's handle (the `SHA-256` of the identifier) is in the
     relay's revocation set;
  3. extract the payment hash from the macaroon identifier and verify that
     `SHA-256(preimage)` equals it;
  4. evaluate the request-independent caveats — `relay`, `valid_until`,
     `valid_after` — and reject any caveat condition the relay does not recognize
     (fail closed; see [Caveats](#caveats)).

If these checks pass the relay accepts the credential (acking it with an `L402-OK`)
and grants the scoped access it authorizes. Because an `L402` message carries no
event or subscription, the **request-dependent** caveats — the remaining scope
conditions (`kinds`, `authors`, `operations`, `tag_<letter>`, `tag_keys`,
`max_event_size`) and the metered conditions — are enforced per subsequent gated
message (`EVENT`, `REQ`, `COUNT`, and `search`/`sync` per their operation): the
relay applies them to each and rejects one that falls outside the credential with
`restricted:` (or `auth-required:` for an `authors=self` credential before NIP-42
AUTH, `payment-required:` when a consumable budget is exhausted, or `rate-limited:` /
`subscription-limit-reached:` when a `rate` / `subscriptions` ceiling is hit).

Access lasts only while the credential remains valid, not for the lifetime of the
connection. A relay MUST re-check `valid_until` and the consumable budgets as it
serves requests; when one no longer holds — `valid_until` passes, or a budget runs
out mid-connection — the relay stops honoring the credential and answers the next
gated action with a fresh `payment-required:` challenge, or, for a read budget that
drains while a subscription is still streaming, closes that subscription with the
challenge (see `read_events`). On a new connection the
client re-presents the stored credential, which grants the same access for as long
as it is still valid. A relay can also revoke a credential before expiry by listing
its handle in a revocation set (checked in step 2 above); because keys are
derived rather than stored, revocation is this list rather than a deleted key. A
revoked handle MUST remain listed until at least its `valid_until` (indefinitely
if it has none), so relays SHOULD set a `valid_until` on every credential to keep
the revocation set bounded.

Rotating the relay master secret changes every derived root key, so credentials
minted under the old secret would otherwise stop verifying — a silent mass
revocation. To rotate without cutting off paying clients, a relay retains its retired
master secrets and tries them at verification (the current secret first, then retired
ones in turn); a credential still verifies under whichever secret minted it. A retired
secret can be dropped once the last credential minted under it has passed its
`valid_until` — a further reason to set one — so outstanding credentials keep working
until they expire while newly minted ones use the current secret.

## Credential state on a connection

A credential accepted with `L402-OK` is remembered for the connection, so the client
does not re-present it on each gated message (it MAY re-present the same credential,
which changes nothing). The relay honors it only while it remains valid — once
`valid_until` passes or a budget is exhausted it stops being honored (see
[Verification](#verification)), and the next gated action gets a fresh
`payment-required:` challenge. A connection MAY hold several credentials at once, just
as a [NIP-42](42.md) session may hold several authenticated pubkeys — for example one
credential scoped to reads and another to writes.

A gated message carries no credential reference, so the relay selects one itself: the
message is authorized if at least one credential held on the connection permits it
under its caveats and has the budget it requires, and the relay charges that
credential. If more than one held credential would authorize the message, the relay
charges exactly one of them; which one is implementation-defined. A client that needs
a particular credential's budget spent therefore SHOULD present only that credential,
use credentials with non-overlapping scope, or present each credential on its own
connection — a connection holding a single credential has no selection ambiguity.
Because metered counters are keyed on the credential handle and aggregated across all
connections (see [Caveats](#caveats)), splitting credentials across connections does
not change what is charged in total; it only removes the ambiguity over which
credential pays.

Credential state is per-connection, but the metered counters it draws on are not:
they persist with the credential handle across reconnects, so re-presenting a
credential on a later connection resumes its remaining budget rather than resetting
it.

## Payment proof and delegation

The preimage is what makes verification stateless. The macaroon identifier commits
to a specific invoice's payment hash, and on Lightning the only way to obtain a
preimage `r` with `SHA-256(r)` equal to that hash is to pay the invoice. A valid
preimage is therefore unforgeable evidence the invoice was paid, checkable with a
single hash and without consulting the relay's Lightning node or a settled-invoice
database. The preimage is the bridge that binds a Lightning payment to the bearer
credential; without it the macaroon would be an unpaid capability and the relay
would be back to tracking settlement state in a database.

The preimage is not an access-control secret and carries no authority of its own —
all authority lives in the macaroon's signature (keyed by a secret only the relay
holds) and in its caveats. A holder can attenuate a macaroon by adding caveats without
the root key, but can neither remove a caveat nor forge a broader macaroon. A
credential can therefore be delegated by attenuating it and handing on
`macaroon:preimage`: the delegate obtains exactly the narrowed scope and cannot
escalate, and revealing the preimage grants nothing further — it only satisfies
the payment check for that one credential, and is in any case already known to
every routing node that forwarded the payment. The value a delegator must protect
is the macaroon, specifically the un-attenuated one, never the preimage.

Because the preimage check is `SHA-256(preimage)` against the committed payment
hash alone, with no binding to the `token_id`, a relay MUST issue a distinct
invoice, with its own payment hash, for every macaroon it mints, so that a preimage
can only ever satisfy the one credential it was minted for.

In L402 the `token_id` is a stable per-user identifier across macaroon rotations
(re-minting a credential to change a service tier, say, keeps the same `token_id`).
This NIP defines no such rotation and requires a unique payment hash per
credential, so each identifier is already unique without it; `token_id` is kept
only for L402 macaroon-format conformance and does no independent work here.

## Paying the invoice

A client obtains the preimage by paying the relay-issued invoice over Lightning.
For a BOLT 11 invoice, [NIP-47](47.md) `pay_invoice` is the expected programmatic
path: the client hands the invoice to its wallet service and receives the
`preimage` in the response. Clients SHOULD check the invoice amount against a
configurable maximum, and against the `fees` advertised in NIP-11, before paying.

The invoice may be BOLT 11 or BOLT 12; the macaroon commits to its payment hash
either way, and recipient privacy via blinded paths is available with both formats.
BOLT 12 needs wallet support beyond NIP-47's BOLT 11 `pay_invoice`, and in return
offers an optional payment receipt (see [Binding to a single
party](#binding-to-a-single-party)).

## Invoice lifecycle and retries

The macaroon in a challenge commits to one invoice's payment hash, and that invoice
carries its own expiry (the BOLT 11 `expiry`, or the BOLT 12 equivalent). The client
MUST pay before the invoice expires; a Lightning payment to an expired invoice fails,
so no preimage is ever obtained for it.

Invoice expiry bounds only when a credential can be *acquired*, not how long it lasts
once paid. A preimage obtained by paying in time satisfies `SHA-256(preimage) ==
payment_hash` indefinitely, so the credential's lifetime is governed by its
`valid_until` / `valid_after` caveats (see [Caveats](#caveats)), not by the original
invoice's expiry.

If the invoice expires unpaid, or the payment fails, that macaroon is permanently
unusable — its payment hash can never be satisfied — and the client discards it. The
macaroon cannot be re-invoiced, because its payment hash is fixed in the signed
identifier; a retry is always a fresh challenge, a new macaroon bound to a new
invoice. A client obtains one by resending the gated action (`EVENT`, `REQ`, …), to
which the relay replies with a new `payment-required:` challenge exactly as on the
first attempt.

Because verification is stateless and keys are derived, the relay keeps no
per-invoice state: it does not track which invoices are outstanding or settled, and
an abandoned (expired-unpaid) macaroon simply never returns. The relay learns a
credential was paid only when the client presents the preimage, which is itself the
proof — no settlement lookup is needed.

## Binding to a single party

The credential is a bearer token by default — the preimage proves the invoice was
paid, not who paid it — which is what makes it reusable and delegable. The `authors`
caveat narrows this, but it binds *scope*, not who may hold the token:

  - `authors=self` plus [NIP-42](42.md) ties the credential's authority to the
    pubkey that authenticates on the connection — the holder can act only as a
    pubkey it can authenticate as. The token is still bearer (anyone may present
    it); what is pinned is the identity its actions run under, not who holds it.
  - `authors=<pubkey>` fixes the credential to a specific author. For writes this
    limits real use to the party that can sign as that pubkey, since only it can
    produce valid events; for reads it restricts which author's events the filters
    may request.

A relay that additionally wants a non-repudiable record of *who paid* — for
receipts, accounting, or refunds — MAY rely on BOLT 12: because the relay issues
the invoice, it receives the payer's signed `invoice_request`, which binds the
payment to the payer's `invreq_payer_id` key and serves as a payment receipt. This
is optional and entirely relay-side — a payment-side artifact separate from access
control — so it adds nothing to the protocol defined here.

## Compatibility with L402 over HTTP

A relay that also exposes an HTTP interface MAY issue macaroons usable over both
transports. For backwards compatibility with older L402 clients, such a relay
MUST accept both the `L402` and `LSAT` scheme names in HTTP `Authorization`
headers, as the L402 specification's backwards-compatibility rule requires. The nostr `L402` message carries the
credential string directly and does not repeat a scheme name.

## Relation to NIP-42

This NIP is independent of [NIP-42](42.md) and does not require it. A relay MAY use
both: NIP-42 to establish which pubkey is connected, and this NIP to require
payment for specific actions. Because the L402 credential is a bearer token and is
not bound to a pubkey, it composes with but does not depend on NIP-42
authentication.

## Rationale and prior work

L402 is specified for HTTP and for gRPC. Because a nostr relay exposes no
per-message HTTP exchange once its WebSocket is open, this NIP treats the relay
message protocol as a third L402 transport, carrying the challenge and credential
in protocol messages the same way the gRPC binding carries them in metadata and
trailers. The L402 core — the macaroon identifier, the caveats, and the preimage
proof — is reused unchanged.

A 2023 draft sketched macaroon-based relay access by adding an LSAT challenge to a
NIP-42 authentication event, but left the preimage proof, the caveats, the
on-the-wire credential format, and verification unspecified. That sketch informally
used the "402" number this NIP now takes; this NIP completes the idea, and
deliberately does not carry it in the authentication flow.

Payment is kept separate from NIP-42 authentication. Community discussion of
payment-bound relay access (see the [auth-by-payment
proposal](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/1609)) favored a dedicated
message over extending `AUTH`, on the grounds that authentication answers "who is
calling" while payment answers "what has been paid for". This NIP follows that
guidance: `AUTH` ([NIP-42](42.md)) remains identity, and `L402` is a separate,
pubkey-independent credential.

Lightning is the settlement network here because it is the common denominator for
Bitcoin payments. Other bearer instruments used for relay access on nostr, such as
Cashu ecash, can be melted to Lightning, so a Lightning-settled credential
interoperates with them rather than competing. What the macaroon adds over a bare
bearer token is stateless scope: caveats attenuate one credential to specific
kinds, a validity window, or a usage budget, and any holder can further restrict
(but never widen) it without contacting the relay.

## Security considerations

  - **Relay secret.** The relay master secret that keys the macaroon HMAC MUST
    remain secret; anyone holding it can mint or forge any credential. Signature
    comparison MUST be constant-time — use a constant-time equality primitive (for
    example Go's `hmac.Equal` or Node's `crypto.timingSafeEqual`), never a plain
    `==`, so the check cannot leak the signature through a timing side channel.
  - **Caveats are the access control.** Attenuation is enforced by the verifier,
    not by the macaroon bytes, so every relay that accepts a credential MUST
    reject (not skip) any caveat condition it does not recognize, and MUST treat
    repeated conditions of the same type as further restrictions, never
    replacements. Two replay threats follow from the bearer design and have
    different defenses:
    - *A relay that does not share the issuer's master secret* re-derives a
      different root key, so the signature check fails and the credential grants
      nothing there, whatever its caveats.
    - *Relays that do share the master secret* (for example a cluster) can all
      verify it, so they MUST apply identical caveat enforcement and enforce
      revocation and the metered counters against a shared, strongly-consistent
      store — with the same atomic check-and-update per credential required of a
      single relay (see [Caveats](#caveats)), now applied across nodes. A merely
      replicated copy is not enough: if two siblings read a counter before either
      writes its update the credential overspends, and a just-revoked credential
      keeps working on a sibling until replication converges. A cluster that cannot
      provide strong consistency SHOULD serialize each credential's metered
      operations to a single node; a cluster that does neither does not meet the
      atomic requirement above, and its asynchronous replication permits bounded
      overspend and brief use-after-revocation until it converges. Such
      credentials SHOULD also carry a `relay=` caveat so that, even among siblings,
      one is honored only at the URL it was minted for rather than cluster-wide.
  - **Bearer semantics.** The credential is a bearer token by design: anyone
    holding `macaroon:preimage` has the access it grants. It is transmitted over
    the already-encrypted `wss` connection, but clients that treat a credential as
    reusable should store it as a secret. Re-presenting a stored credential on a
    new connection is intended and is not a second payment. A relay that needs to
    bind access to a single party can add a caveat tying the credential to a
    pubkey and require NIP-42 for that pubkey.
  - **Revocation is silent.** A revoked paid credential yields the same
    `payment-required:` response as ordinary expiry, so a client cannot tell a
    relay revoking a still-valid credential from a normal top-up prompt. Operators
    should weigh the trust and refund implications, and surface revocation out of
    band where it needs to be auditable.
  - **Privacy.** L402 binds payment to credential: the macaroon commits to the
    invoice's payment hash and carries a stable identifier that the relay — the
    invoice's issuer and the credential's verifier — sees on every reuse, so it can
    correlate a payer's payment with their sessions. Blind-signed ecash instead
    unlinks payment from use (the mint cannot match a spent token to its issuance),
    giving stronger payer privacy. Operators and clients that need it should
    consider an ecash-based payment method (for example [NIP-61](61.md)); defining
    such a method is out of scope for this NIP.
